## Introduction

Most chess engines pair an evaluation with a deep search. This note asks a narrower question: how well does a small network play when it only gets one look at the board? Jev-chess small reads the position once and returns a probability for every legal move. It plays the most likely one.

The design follows the public description of Jev: a discriminative transformer that makes a single-pass *Choice* over at most 255 options. Chess has at most 218 legal moves, so the choice always fits. Jev's internals are unpublished. This is an independent model, not their weights.

## Model

The board is always encoded from the side to move. Black positions are mirrored. The input is 65 tokens: one for each of the 64 squares (empty, or one of 12 piece kinds) and one meta token that holds the four castling rights and the en-passant file.

An 8-layer encoder ($d = 256$, 8 heads) processes the tokens. A bilinear head scores every from–to pair:

$$
s_{ij} = \frac{(W_q h_i)^\top (W_k h_j)}{\sqrt{d}}
$$

Underpromotions get 24 extra slots read off the from-square, for 4,168 move logits in total. A softmax over only the legal moves gives the policy.

## Results

| Metric | Value |
| ------ | ----- |
| Parameters | 6.5M |
| Training | 30,000 steps, 5M positions [1] |
| Held-out top-1 | 36.5% |
| Calibration ECE | 0.036 |
| Elo (CCRL scale) | 1239 ± 51 |

Elo was estimated from 200 games against Stockfish limited to `UCI_Elo` 1320 and 1400, at 60s+0.6s. Because there is no search, the time control does not matter to Jev.

## Conclusion

A single forward pass with 6.5M parameters reaches roughly 1240 Elo with well-calibrated move probabilities. Search is the obvious next step, and the calibrated policy is a natural prior for it.
